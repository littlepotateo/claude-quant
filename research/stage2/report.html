The Nikkei → DAX → Nasdaq session idea does not survive real data

I rebuilt the strategy from free Yahoo index data, 2001–2026, with trading costs. 0 of 13 pre-registered tests passed. The rule closest to the repo's description lost money out-of-sample. A S$5,000 account trading it with micro futures would have been wiped out by April 2018.

Why it fails: the gains happen while these markets are closed

The strategy only holds positions during each market's session, from open to close. Since 2001, almost all of the long-run rise in these indices came between one session's close and the next session's open. A strategy that is flat overnight starts from that handicap. It needs a real directional edge just to break even, and the signal isn't strong enough.

Every rule, tested the same way

The rules, threshold, VIX bands, cost and pass criteria were fixed before any result was seen. Each signal uses only prices known before the trade opens. A rule had to pass all five checks: out-of-sample Sharpe above 0.5 after 5 bps, beating always-long, beating 95% of 1,000 random-direction runs, an in-sample t-stat above 2.89 (the 5% level after a Bonferroni correction for 13 tests), and staying positive in the 2025–2026 hold-out. Costs decide it

Before costs, the combined lead-lag rule has an out-of-sample Sharpe of +0.40. At 2 bps per trade it is already negative. Trading three sessions a day multiplies the cost drag, and a weak signal can't carry it.

The one lead worth noting

Trading the Nikkei session in the direction of the previous Nasdaq session, only when the Nasdaq move was bigger than 0.25%, is the strongest result:

  • In-sample Sharpe +0.61, t-stat +2.5. That's below the 2.89 needed once you account for 13 tests.
  • Out-of-sample Sharpe +0.22, under the 0.5 pass line. It breaks even at 6.5 bps.
  • Hold-out Sharpe +1.73 over 438 days. That's encouraging but short, and one good stretch is what chance produces when you test 13 rules.

This is the US-to-Japan spillover documented in academic work. Most of it shows up in the Nikkei's opening gap, before you can trade. What's left is thin.

What would have made it look good

Using the full same-day DAX session as the Nasdaq signal turns the Nasdaq leg into a Sharpe of +1.95 in-sample and +1.35 out-of-sample. That signal isn't tradable: the DAX closes two hours after the Nasdaq opens. A timing slip this small is enough to produce an impressive-looking backtest, which is why the tests above check that every signal comes before its trade.

The hourly data confirms the timing matters, and that the honest version fails. Rebuilding the Nasdaq leg from 60-minute bars over 2023-12-06 to 2026-10-22, using DAX prices only up to 14:00 Berlin, gives a Sharpe of -1.10, against -1.67 for the daily proxy. The two versions take the same side 78% of the time.

"Sequential compounding" is leverage

The README says rolling the full account through three sessions a day is 4.75× more capital-efficient than splitting it. Out-of-sample, the sequential version has the same Sharpe as an even split held at three times the size:

Compounding magnifies whatever the edge is. With a negative edge it magnifies losses.

What it means for a S$5,000 account

Futures come in whole contracts, so you can't size down to match a small account. One contract of each micro future today is a very different bet:

Smallest contractExposure per contract todayTimes a S$5,000 account
Nikkei 225 Micro (OSE)S$5,1211.0×
Micro-DAX FDXS (Eurex)S$36,3547.3×
MNQ (CME)S$73,98414.8×

Trading the combined lead-lag rule with one contract per session from January 2017, at 5 bps cost and today's exchange rates, the account fell to zero in April 2018. With no costs at all it still hit zero, in November 2022. One contract is a fixed bet, so as the account shrinks the leverage rises, and losses speed up.

Margin calls aren't modelled; a broker would have closed positions earlier. Index levels in 2017 were lower, so the starting exposure was 2.5× for MNQ and 3.3× for Micro-DAX.

Recommendation

  • Don't trade this strategy, and don't pay for futures data to test it further. It fails the criteria agreed before the test, and with whole contracts the account is too small to size it safely.
  • If you want to keep researching, the only thread left is the Nikkei-after-Nasdaq rule. Treat it as a new hypothesis, test it on genuinely new data (futures, not cash index), and paper trade it before any money goes in.
  • Remember the overnight effect. Strategies that are flat overnight give up most of the market's historical return. This is general information, not personal financial advice.

Method and limits

  • Data: Yahoo Finance daily bars for ^N225, ^GDAXI, ^NDX and ^VIX, 2000–2026, fetched 2026-10-23T03:17:16+00:00, plus 60-minute bars for 2023–2026. The Nasdaq series starts 2001 to skip defective opens in 2000. The 44 days where Yahoo reports an open equal to the prior close are not traded.
  • Signals: the Nikkei uses the previous Nasdaq session. The DAX uses the same day's Nikkei session. The Nasdaq uses the same day's Nikkei session plus the DAX opening gap. VIX scaling uses the prior VIX close.
  • Prices: cash-index open and close are a proxy. Futures trade around them, and real fills will differ.
  • Contracts: MNQ is $2 × Nasdaq 100 (CME), Micro-DAX is €1 × DAX (Eurex), Nikkei 225 Micro is ¥10 × Nikkei (OSE). FX is held at EURUSD 1.1217, USDJPY 156.912, USDSGD 1.2774.
  • Reproduce: research/stage2/ in the repo: fetch_data.py, backtest.py, make_report.py, and 11 unit tests including lookahead checks.
